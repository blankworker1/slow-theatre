# Doing the Work

**Doing the work** is the practice of acquiring understanding through
direct, repeated, personal experience, in cases where that
understanding cannot be transferred by explanation alone. It has no
substitute: this kind of understanding cannot be delivered, only
earned.

The underlying reason is what philosopher Michael Polanyi called
**tacit knowledge** — knowledge that exists in the doing of a thing
and cannot be fully stated, summarised in his own phrase: *we can know
more than we can tell.*

## Example

Most cyclists cannot correctly describe *countersteering* — a brief
push on the right handlebar to turn left — despite performing it
correctly on every ride, from the first day they could ride at all.
Told the physics beforehand, a new rider still cannot ride; the
knowledge is acquired only by riding, falling, and correcting, and
often remains impossible to state accurately even once fully
acquired.

## Related instances

- Craft apprenticeship, historically transmitted through years of
  supervised practice rather than written instruction.
- Comic timing, which no script or note can specify in advance —
  acquired only through repeated performance in front of a live
  audience.

## Applied here

Every document behind this one — the manifesto, the essays, the
boundaries and protocols governing La Prova — can be read carefully,
in full, and might still only convey half of what they describe. The
reading is real, and worth doing properly rather than skimmed. But
La Prova is built around gift, barter, and promise specifically
because those are practices, not facts. 
The documents are trying to explain this in words. They  do not tell you
what it feels like to stand at a table of your own things and actually resist pricing them, until you've done it yourself. The documents are the map. Doing the work is the territory. No map, however complete, is the territory itself.
