# BosaTAZ — Boundaries

*Part of the TAZ folder — where the mechanics of BosaTAZ, the local
project, get built. A growing companion to Slow Theatre — Exercises
(kept general, at the repository root, since exercises like
RESERVATO are useful to any Node) — not a fixed list, but a first
account of what holds this Node's edges in place. This is Bosa's own
worked example, not a claim about what every Slow Theatre Node's
boundaries must be — the manifesto's own question ("what actually
holds your boundaries up — infrastructure, law, or only people
choosing to respect them?") is what every Node answers for itself;
this document is Bosa's answer to it, specific to BosaTAZ and to
Italian law, not a template to inherit unchanged.

## Why boundaries need naming at all

A zone that announces itself with no sign, no ticket, and no gate
still has to end somewhere — otherwise "zone" stops meaning
anything. The interesting question is never *whether* a boundary
exists, but *what kind* it is, and what actually holds it up when
nobody's enforcing it. Some boundaries are simply presence. Some are
digital. Some are legal. Some exist only because a group of people,
independently, keep choosing to respect them. La Prova itself runs
on all four at once, and they're worth naming separately, because
each one fails in a different way and needs a different kind of
attention to stay intact.

## The physical boundary

The most fundamental of the four, and the one every other boundary
in this document is really an extension of. There are no fences, no
tickets, no guest list. The only boundary is presence itself: be
there, or be elsewhere. Nothing checks anyone in, nothing confirms
anyone belongs, nothing stops anyone from simply walking up. This is
the "low threshold" principle already built into Act One, Three, and
Four — arriving is the entire requirement — named here as a boundary
in its own right rather than just a design preference.

What holds it up is the simplest possible thing: geography. Nobody
enforces it, because nothing needs to. A person is either standing
in the space where La Prova is happening, or they aren't, and no
infrastructure, law, or restraint is required to make that true.
It's also the boundary every other one in this document quietly
assumes — the digital, fiscal, and mental boundaries all only matter
for someone who has already crossed this one first.

## The digital boundary

The radius of the signal — the perimeter actually covered by
BosaTAZ's network. Whoever is inside the signal is inside the zone;
whoever is outside, isn't. Nothing announces this boundary on
purpose. The phone does it unprompted, reporting "no internet
connection" the moment it joins — the zone's own edge, confirmed by
a stranger's device rather than a sign anyone had to put up. In a
sense, this is the physical boundary's own younger sibling — the
same "be there or be elsewhere" logic, just extended to a network
instead of a place, and arriving only once BosaTAZ existed to need
it.

This boundary is one of the easiest to verify and one of the hardest
to argue with. It either includes you or it doesn't, and no
persuasion changes which.

## The fiscal boundary

The legal perimeter: only barter and gift, never a sale, never a
cash top-up. Unlike the physical or digital boundaries, this one
isn't drawn by geography or infrastructure — it's drawn by law, and
it has to hold up in front of someone who isn't inclined to take it
on faith: an official, an inspection, a skeptical reader of the
rules. That's why it's written in the most precise, verifiable
language of the four — precision is the boundary's actual material,
not a nice-to-have.

This boundary fails differently than the physical or digital ones.
It doesn't fail by someone walking through it; it fails by someone
quietly redefining what's happening inside it — a conguaglio here, a
pattern of habitual trading there — until what was legally a gift or
a barter has become, without anyone announcing it, a sale.

## The mental boundary

The least visible of the four, and the one that makes the others
real. This is the boundary of behaviour: inside the zone, the very
idea of "buying" shouldn't even surface as an available thought. A
truck with no network and a law about barter can both be perfectly
true, and someone inside the perimeter can still be thinking like
they're at the supermarket. The mental boundary is what actually
closes the space off, and — unlike the others — it isn't backed by
any geography, infrastructure, or statute at all. It's held up
entirely by individuals choosing, moment to moment, not to reach.

This is the boundary Il lupo perde il pelo ma non il vizio is
about, and the one Exercises trains directly — RESERVATO, in
particular, rehearses a close relative of it: not the vice of
pricing things, but the vice of feeling owed an explanation for
someone else's line. Both are the same category of boundary, held
up by the same kind of muscle.

## What these four have in common, and where they differ

None of the four boundaries is stronger than the others on its
own — a zone with perfect open presence, a clean signal perimeter,
and a watertight legal argument still isn't a zone at all if
everyone inside it is privately thinking in prices. All four work
together: the physical boundary gives the other three somewhere to
happen at all, the digital boundary extends that same presence-logic
onto BosaTAZ specifically, the fiscal boundary gives the space
something precise to be legally true to, and the mental boundary is
what actually makes the other three mean anything in practice rather
than on paper.

## The identity boundary

A different kind of discovery from the first four, worth naming
precisely rather than folding in as a fifth instance of the same
pattern. The first four all govern the same thing — being present at
La Prova, simultaneously, in the same place. This one governs
something that happens later, elsewhere, and only for someone who
chooses it: the crossing from reading BosaNode's public Discussions
to posting in them. Low threshold in effort — a GitHub account costs
nothing and takes minutes — but genuinely unlike anything upstream
of it in kind, not just in degree.

Every other boundary in this document is built around *not* needing
to be known — the physical boundary's anonymous arrival, the
digital boundary's ephemeral, unlinked keys, the mental boundary's
anonymous restraint, even the fiscal boundary's anonymous donazione.
This is the first point anywhere in the system where a persistent,
named identity is required rather than avoided. Crossing it doesn't
make someone a performer the way taking an object or offering a
barter does — that kind of performing is instant and fully
reversible, done the moment it's done. This crossing is more
lasting: not performing *in* a rehearsal, but starting to shape how
future ones get built.

What holds this boundary up is also unlike the other four, worth
being honest about: not geography, not infrastructure, not law, not
an individual choosing restraint moment to moment — it's a private
platform's own account system, sitting entirely outside the
project's control. Nobody designed this boundary. It exists because
GitHub requires it for anyone to participate, and the project simply
inherited it at the one point its own arc happens to touch that
platform.

## What these five have in common, and where the fifth stands apart

All five exist for the same underlying reason: a zone with no sign,
no ticket, and no gate still needs something holding its edges in
place, even when nobody's enforcing them. But the identity boundary
doesn't slot into the "four work together" picture above it the way
the others do — it isn't active at the same time or in the same
place as the physical, digital, fiscal, and mental boundaries, and
none of the four depends on it, or is strengthened by it, the way
they depend on and strengthen each other. It's downstream and
separate: a boundary for a different stage of the same project,
governing not who gets to be at La Prova, but who gets to help shape
what La Prova becomes next.

---

*This is a first account, not a complete one. Other zones —
different performances, different Nodes, different rehearsals —
will likely surface boundaries of their own that don't fit neatly
into these five. This document exists to be returned to and
expanded, not treated as finished.*
