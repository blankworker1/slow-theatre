# BosaTAZ — Boundaries

*A growing companion, alongside Exercises — not a fixed list, but a
first account of what holds a zone's edges in place. Every zone has
boundaries somewhere; this document is where they're named, one at
a time, as they're identified.*

## Why boundaries need naming at all

A zone that announces itself with no sign, no ticket, and no gate
still has to end somewhere — otherwise "zone" stops meaning
anything. The interesting question is never *whether* a boundary
exists, but *what kind* it is, and what actually holds it up when
nobody's enforcing it. Some boundaries are physical. Some are legal.
Some exist only because a group of people, independently, keep
choosing to respect them. La Prova runs on all three at once, and
they're worth naming separately, because each one fails in a
different way and needs a different kind of attention to stay
intact.

## The physical boundary

The radius of the signal — the perimeter actually covered by
BosaTAZ's network. Whoever is inside the signal is inside the zone;
whoever is outside, isn't. Nothing announces this boundary on
purpose. The phone does it unprompted, reporting "no internet
connection" the moment it joins — the zone's own edge, confirmed by
a stranger's device rather than a sign anyone had to put up.

This boundary is the easiest of the three to verify and the hardest
to argue with. It either includes you or it doesn't, and no
persuasion changes which.

## The fiscal boundary

The legal perimeter: only barter and gift, never a sale, never a
cash top-up. Unlike the physical boundary, this one isn't drawn by
infrastructure — it's drawn by law, and it has to hold up in front
of someone who isn't inclined to take it on faith: an official, an
inspection, a skeptical reader of the rules. That's why it's written
in the most precise, verifiable language of the three — precision
is the boundary's actual material, not a nice-to-have.

This boundary fails differently than the physical one. It doesn't
fail by someone walking through it; it fails by someone quietly
redefining what's happening inside it — a conguaglio here, a
pattern of habitual trading there — until what was legally a gift or
a barter has become, without anyone announcing it, a sale.

## The mental boundary

The least visible of the three, and the one that makes the other
two real. This is the boundary of behaviour: inside the zone, the
very idea of "buying" shouldn't even surface as an available
thought. A truck with no network and a law about barter can both be
perfectly true, and someone inside the perimeter can still be
thinking like they're at the supermarket. The mental boundary is
what actually closes the space off, and — unlike the other two — it
isn't backed by any infrastructure or statute at all. It's held up
entirely by individuals choosing, moment to moment, not to reach.

This is the boundary Il lupo perde il pelo ma non il vizio is
about, and the one Exercises trains directly — RESERVATO, in
particular, rehearses a close relative of it: not the vice of
pricing things, but the vice of feeling owed an explanation for
someone else's line. Both are the same category of boundary, held
up by the same kind of muscle.

## What these three have in common, and where they differ

None of the three boundaries is stronger than the others on its
own — a zone with a perfect signal perimeter and a watertight legal
argument still isn't a zone at all if everyone inside it is
privately thinking in prices. The three only work together: the
physical boundary gives the mental one somewhere to happen, the
fiscal boundary gives it something precise to be true to, and the
mental boundary is what actually makes the other two mean anything
in practice rather than on paper.

---

*This is a first account, not a complete one. Other zones —
different performances, different Nodes, different rehearsals —
will likely surface boundaries of their own that don't fit neatly
into these three. This document exists to be returned to and
expanded, not treated as finished.*
