# Accretive Theatre

Accretion is a geological word before it's anything else — the process
by which a coral reef, a mountain, a river delta builds itself, one
layer at a time, with no single moment anyone could point to and say
*finished*. Nothing about a reef is incomplete while it's growing. It's
simply as much reef as it's had time to become.

Accretive theatre borrows the same idea and asks a performance to work
the same way. Not staged in full, from a finished script, on an opening
night. Built the way the reef is built — one real layer at a time, each
one complete enough to stand on its own, with no promise about how many
more layers there will ever be.

## A precedent already standing

Barcelona's Sagrada Família broke ground on 19 March 1882. On 7
November 2010 — a hundred and twenty-eight years later, and still
visibly, audibly under construction around the congregation — it was
consecrated and opened for worship. People have prayed inside it,
continuously, for over a decade before its central tower was even
finished; that tower wasn't completed until February 2026, a hundred
and forty-four years after the first stone. At no point in that century
and a half was the building "not really a church yet." It was exactly
as much church as it had been built to be, doing exactly what a church
does, the whole time.

That's the precedent worth holding onto, because it answers the
question accretive theatre keeps having to answer: is an incomplete
thing lesser, or is it just honest about its own age? The Sagrada
Família's answer has been standing in Barcelona for over a century.
Nobody visiting it in 1950, or 1980, or 2010, needed to be told it
wasn't finished. They could see the scaffolding. What they experienced
inside it was still completely real.

## Where this project was already doing it

The word is new. The practice isn't — accretion turns out to be the
principle this project has been running on at every level, arrived at
separately each time, never named as one thing until now.

- **The chain object** grows one trade at a time, never complete,
  never meant to be.
- **The back panel** fills one photograph at a time, retires at a
  hundred, and starts again.
- **The Carta de Logu** is being copied one page per rehearsal,
  deliberately over years, because "the practice itself remains the
  point."
- **The satellite roles** were added to the design one at a time —
  Il Trasformatore, then La Sarta, then Il Custode, then Il Suonatore —
  across months of separate conversations, never all at once.
- **The performance structure itself** now follows the same rule
  deliberately: the first rehearsal is Lo Spuntino and Act One alone —
  "1+1" — run for as long as it takes to feel solid, with no fixed
  floor and no promised ceiling, before anything further is added.

## What it asks of the documentation

If the performance is honest about being incomplete, the record of it
has to be too. A document describing all four acts, every satellite
role, every protocol, before a single one of them has actually been
rehearsed, isn't more complete — it's just further ahead of the truth
than the people reading it. Accretive theatre's documentation grows the
same way the performance does: what's public reflects what's actually
been run, nothing previewed, nothing implied ahead of when it's real.

## What it isn't

Not an excuse for being unfinished, and not a promise to eventually be
finished either. The Sagrada Família was never "eventually" a church —
it was one from the day the doors opened, scaffolding and all.
Accretive theatre makes the same claim: each layer, once it's real, is
complete on its own terms. What comes next is simply what comes next,
whenever it's actually ready to.

1+1 does not have to equal anything.
