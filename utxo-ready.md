# UTXO Ready

An open series of artifacts made by Il Trasformatore, each with the
capacity to hold Bitcoin, most of which never will. No number decided in
advance, no edition size, no curator choosing which pieces matter. What
each one becomes is discovered, not designed.

## What each piece is

A sealed steel plate carrying twelve words — a Bitcoin seed — physically
embedded and hidden inside an object made at the Transformation Station,
during an entirely ordinary Trasformazione turn. Nothing about the
making itself is exceptional; the plate arrives already sealed, from a
separate ceremony (below), and simply becomes part of whatever's being
made that day.

The only marking on the object: **"UTXO ready."** Nothing else. Not a
claim that value is inside — most pieces will hold nothing, most of the
time — only that the capacity exists. Beside it, a QR code for the
object's public key (or xpub), so anyone, at any time, can verify
exactly what it currently holds, independently, with no one's word
required.

No visual uniformity is required or expected between pieces, and none
should be manufactured — Il Trasformatore's existing Visual Protocol
(visible seams, a real hand, one imperfection left uncorrected) still
governs how each is made. The marker text is the only thing any two
pieces in this family necessarily share.

## The Keymaker ceremony

Before a plate is embedded in anything, it's made — publicly, witnessed,
using the same protocol already built for btckeys: the Entropia drum and
pill-holder ceremony, SeedSigner for the checksum, Seed Hammer for the
engraving. Multiple people each contribute entropy, so that no single
person — including whoever facilitates the ceremony — ever holds the
complete seed alone. "Owned by everyone and no one" is a literal,
verifiable fact here, not a description.

**Il Keymaker** is the role that facilitates this — anonymous, in the
same tradition as the rest of Anonymous Art. Anonymity here governs
credit, not visibility: the ceremony itself is open to watch in full;
nobody is named or celebrated afterward.

The plate is sealed in plastic as the ceremony's final step. Only once
sealed does it leave the ceremony and, at a later rehearsal, become
Il Trasformatore's material for an entirely ordinary piece.

## Two-stage custody

Value and risk aren't evenly distributed across the family, and custody
doesn't pretend otherwise:

- **Below the threshold** — rotating custody with Il Custode, exactly
  like the chain object: no ceremony, brought to the next rehearsal,
  placed back on display.
- **Above the threshold** — the piece stops rotating. It moves to its
  own permanent plinth and stays there, always visible, protected the
  way the Remembrancer's bound volumes are protected: by never leaving
  sight, not by being hidden.

**Nobody decides when a piece crosses.** The threshold is a public
number, set once, openly — not adjusted quietly, not a judgment call.
Crossing it is exactly as verifiable as anything else in this family:
anyone can check the balance against the public key at any time. When
it happens, it happens; nobody curates it into being.

The move to a permanent plinth *is* the marker of that transition — no
chalk line, no announcement, consistent with "objects mark transitions,
not costumes or announcements" already governing everything else here.

## Why an open series, and not a limited edition

A limited edition is scarcity decided in advance, by someone, before
anything exists — a number chosen, a market shaped. Nothing here works
that way. There's no cap on how many UTXO-ready pieces ever get made;
it's simply however many accumulate, one rehearsal at a time, for as
long as this continues. What makes a given piece eventually matter isn't
curated either — it emerges from real, voluntary generosity over time,
crossing a threshold anyone can check independently. No gatekeeper
decides what's valuable here, the same argument already running through
permuta and donazione, now showing up in which object gets to matter.

## Why multiplicity, not a decoy

The idea started as a decoy — something to protect the real
one by displaying something false. That doesn't fit anywhere else this
project's ever landed: everything here withholds by not explaining,
never by actively misleading. A decoy needs a lie to work.

Genuine multiplicity gets the same security property honestly. Many
real, honestly-labelled pieces exist at once, most low-value, most
places at once, with no single object an obvious target and no secret
"tell" to discover — every one of them is exactly what it claims to be.
A determined thief facing a decoy just has to find the trick. One
facing genuine multiplicity has to track and correlate many separate,
fully real objects, a harder and more expensive problem, honestly
built.

## What's still open

The exact threshold number, and the physical security specifics for
pieces approaching it — display case, tamper-evident sealing, any
transit coverage for the rotating stage — are practical questions worth
real, specific expertise before they're settled, not decided in the
abstract here.
