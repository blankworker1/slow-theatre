# Briefing Paper: The Slow Movement Landscape, and Where Slow Theatre Sits

*Internal reference document*

---

## Purpose

This briefing exists to answer one question cleanly: **is Slow Theatre's use of Slow Food's language and framework defensible, or is it just borrowing a trendy name?**

Short answer: defensible. Good and Fair use the same point of reference Slow Food does — judged the same way, for the same underlying reason. Clean uses a different point of reference in this context — the same move Cittaslow already made when it took the word from farms to towns. All three still map onto something real and checkable in how Slow Theatre actually works; none of the three is asserted on vibes alone.

---

## 1. The wider "Slow" ecosystem

Since Slow Food's founding (Carlo Petrini, Italy, 1986 — a protest against a McDonald's opening on Rome's Spanish Steps), the word "Slow" has been attached to a long list of domains: Slow Cities (Cittaslow), Slow Money, Slow Fashion, Slow Travel, Slow Parenting, Slow Schools/Education, Slow Reading, Slow Gardening, Slow Cinema, Slow Art, Slow Media/TV, Slow Science, Slow Technology, Slow Design, Slow Architecture, Slow Ageing, Slow Church, Slow Counselling, Slow Photography, and more.

This list is much longer, and much less exclusive, than it first appears.

---

## 2. Two genuinely different categories

Splitting the list by what it actually claims reveals a real fault line:

**Principle-based** — carries an actual structural/economic critique, not just a mood:
- **Slow Food** — the origin. Good, Clean, Fair as explicit criteria; opposes industrial food production as a system.
- **Slow Money** — patient capital, local investment, soil health as the real return. A genuine alternative financial mechanism.
- **Slow Fashion** — opposes "fast fashion" specifically as a business model; fair trade, artisan-made, anti-mass-production.
- **Cittaslow** — a real certification body (236 towns, 30 countries) with actual admission criteria: heritage preservation, local economy, resident wellbeing.

**Tempo-only** — genuinely just about slowing the pace of an existing activity, no structural critique attached:
Slow Reading, Slow Gardening, Slow Cinema, Slow Art (Slow Art Day — spend longer looking at fewer paintings), Slow Science, Slow Technology, Slow Design, Slow Architecture, Slow Ageing, Slow Parenting, Slow Schools, Slow Church, Slow Counselling, Slow Photography, Slow Media/TV.

**Ambiguous:** Slow Travel has some economic dimension (staying longer supports local businesses) but is rarely articulated as a structural critique — mostly sold as an experience of pace.

**Where Slow Theatre sits:** in the principle-based camp, with a twist. The "patient, recurring, local" tempo isn't decorative — it's the mechanism by which trust builds enough to make barter viable. Slow Theatre is "slow" the way Slow Money is "patient": the pace serves the structural alternative, not the other way around.

---

## 3. Who actually uses the phrase "Good, Clean, Fair"

The results of this question are revealing:

- **Cittaslow** is the only other initiative that directly reuses the literal three-word phrase — and only because it was co-founded (Paolo Saturnini, Orvieto) with Carlo Petrini's direct, immediate support. It inherited the language, not just the spirit.

Other principle-based initiatives reference Slow Food's critique without adopting its exact vocabulary:

- **Slow Money** does not use the phrase. Its own vocabulary is "patient capital," "nurture capital," soil fertility as the real return.
- **Slow Fashion** does not use the phrase either, despite covering similar ethical/economic ground.

**Conclusion:** Slow Theatre reusing "Good, Clean, Fair" verbatim is a rare move — closer to what Cittaslow did (direct inheritance) than what Money or Fashion did (parallel language, same spirit).

No other initiative has applied the literal phrase to a creative or performance domain. This is open ground, not an imitation.

**Why this is legitimate:** Slow Theatre is designed to launch at Slow Food tables — the argument has already been made once, in a language everyone at the table already trusts. The recognition is invited, not assumed.

---

## 4. Testing Slow Theatre's own use of the three words

Each word tested against Slow Food's actual definition, and against what Slow Theatre actually does — not just the general "spirit" of the movement. Rather than ranking the three, this asks a simpler question of each: does Slow Theatre use the word to mean the same thing Slow Food does, or a different thing in this context?

- **Good — same point of reference.**

Slow Food: judged by direct sensory experience — food should taste good, full stop, no institutional approval required.

Slow Theatre: judged by what it feels like to be in the room, not production values or press coverage. Same underlying move — experience over external validation — applied to a different sense (felt presence, not taste).

- **Fair — same point of reference.**

Slow Food: producers fairly compensated, value set through direct relationship rather than an external market authority.

Slow Theatre: barter with no money as third-party price-setter — two people establish value together. The mechanism is structurally identical: removing intermediaries from the exchange.

- **Clean — different point of reference, same move Cittaslow made.**

Slow Food: produced without harming the environment, animal welfare, or human health — specifically about physical production process.

Slow Theatre: no sponsor, no hidden commercial agenda, no extraction from participants — enacted through barter-only exchange and zero external revenue. This is closer to what Slow Food's own "Fair" covers than to "Clean" in its original sense.

**Why this holds up rather than reading as a stretch:** Cittaslow made the identical move first. Its own "Clean" isn't about pesticides — it's environmental policy, heritage preservation, quality-of-life planning for a town. The word already survived one domain jump (food → city) by keeping its *spirit* (don't harm the thing you depend on) while losing its literal content (no chemical inputs). Slow Theatre's redefinition of "Clean" follows the same precedent, not an invented one.

---

## 5. How Slow Theatre differs from obvious comparisons

**Slow Cinema:** Shares temporal duration, but adds structural economic critique. Slow Cinema is about aesthetic experience; Slow Theatre is about alternative behavioral patterns and exchange mechanisms.

**Invisible Theatre (Boal):** Shares public space and blurring performer/audience, but adds recurrence and barter. Boal's theatre is intervention; Slow Theatre is cultivation — the same soil tilled repeatedly.

**Street theatre festivals (Aurillac, Mercantia):** Share scale, location, and European lineage, but reject the festival model. Both are annual gatherings; Slow Theatre is recurring on no fixed calendar, local-capital-only, and non-monetary.

**"Participatory theatre":** Slow Theatre isn't just "accessible theatre" (democratizing a form) but "alternative theatre" (replacing a system's logic: no box office, no season, no closure).

---

## 6. Comparable precedents

Two real, small-town, street-theatre-specific cases that prove the model — and clarify how Slow Theatre differs:

- **Aurillac, France** (est. 1986) — deliberately located in a small, otherwise unremarkable rural town; grew from six theatre companies to over 600, and 100,000+ spectators over four days each August. The world's largest street arts gathering, run by ÉCLAT, the National Centre for Street Arts. **Precedent for:** scale, location, European street theatre tradition.

**Difference from Slow Theatre:** an annual festival with imported companies from outside the town, not a recurring local structure.

- **Mercantia, Certaldo** (Tuscany, Italy) — Italy's leading street theatre festival, now in its 38th year, in a small medieval hilltop town; ~60,000 visitors, ~500 street artists, ~90 companies over five days. **Precedent for:** Italian piazza setting, medieval town as stage, street theatre as cultural institution.

**Difference from Slow Theatre:** a single annual gathering with professional touring companies, not a frequent, undated, participant-driven recurrence.

Both prove street theatre can anchor local culture without major cities. Neither proves the Slow Theatre model — non-monetary, recurring, participant-as-performer — which remains the specific innovation.

---

## Conclusion

**The one-line answer:** Slow Theatre applies Slow Food's framework with the same legitimacy Cittaslow did. Good and Fair use the same point of reference Slow Food itself uses; Clean uses a different one, in the same way and for the same reason Cittaslow's does. The temporal dimension — patient, recurring, local — isn't a decoration on top of this; it's how enough trust builds to make the barter economics actually work.
