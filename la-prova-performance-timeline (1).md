# La Prova — Performance Timeline

*A working document mapping the physical, social, and dramaturgical structure of one rehearsal, from Lo Spuntino through Il Brindisi. Companion to the manifesto, FAQ, roleplay document, and remembrancer.md.*

---

## Premise

Nothing on this timeline is announced. No one hosts, checks anyone in, or explains what is happening. Every transition is a physical, visual event — someone standing up, a box arriving, a truck unloading — recognisable to those already inside the frame, unremarkable to anyone who isn't. Coordination happens only where explicitly noted (via the WhatsApp group, in advance); everything else is unhurried and individually triggered on the day.

Three things everyone is asked to bring, mapping to the day's three registers:

- **Something to share** — food and drink → Lo Spuntino (give, anonymous, consumed)
- **Something to gift** — a box of unwanted objects → L'Usato (give, named, discussed, no return expected)
- **Something to barter** — one item of slightly more value → the Gallery (negotiated, something changes hands both ways)

Nobody loses out. Some people simply go home with something different than they arrived with.

---

## Site

A public *area picnic*, requiring no booking or permission. Ideally adjacent to a small rural bar (toilets), with parking for arriving cars and the truck, and enough open space for the truck, mats, workbench, and Gallery to sit together.

No signage or branding marks the site as a Slow Theatre event.

---

## 0 — Lo Spuntino (pre-performance)

- Eaters arrive at staggered times, no fixed start.
- Food and drink placed on a shared communal table — anonymous, untracked. No pairing, no thanks, no record of who brought or took what.
- The truck is parked out of sight nearby.
- Everyone present is, for now, simply an eater.

---

## Act 1 — Set-up and L'Usato / Trasformazione

**Signal in:** the truck is driven in and parked. Its arrival is ambiguous — not a clear announcement — but recognised by the eaters as an expected event.

**The real signal:** unloading and setup begins. This is visually distinct from ordinary picnic-area activity and is the moment eaters become *aspettatori*. No opt-in, no explanation offered.

Unloaded and set up together, quietly, without show:
- **The floor** — mats laid in a line flanking the truck (matched 1:1 to the agreed number of mat-owners; scalable — the only coordination required is that the count matches)
- **The workbench** — for the Trasformatore/artists
- **The Gallery** — pedestals and easels, positioned a short walk from the workbench, in a distinct zone. Empty to start.

### L'Usato
- Mat-owners, agreed beforehand via WhatsApp, are themselves eaters. Each finishes eating in their own time, collects a box of objects from their car, and lays it out on their mat. No hurry, no coordination beyond the earlier headcount.
- Objects on a mat belong to that owner. The owner narrates, discusses, and gifts them — no payment, no exchange. This is the first rule change from Lo Spuntino: anonymous gift becomes named, discussed gift.

### Trasformazione
- Artists (up to 4 for this first rehearsal) have pre-agreed time slots (~30 min each) and order, arranged in advance via WhatsApp.
- Each artist, in turn, finishes eating, collects their own box of tools/materials from their car, and sets up at the workbench.
- The artist visits each mat in turn, looking and discussing anything that catches their eye, gifted to them by the mat-owner, to be used in the artifact made during their slot.
- At the end of their slot, the artist leaves the workbench and carries the finished piece to the Gallery, placing it on a pedestal or easel.

This cycle — rise, collect, set up, no announcement — repeats at three scales across Act 1: the truck (once), the mat-owners (per mat), and the artists (per slot, twice: once to the workbench, once to the Gallery).

**Act 1 runs roughly the length of the artist slots** (~2 hours for 4×30 min, plus unhurried gaps between).

---

## Act 2 — The Gallery / Barter

**Threshold:** not a gradual fill, but a hard cut — the final artist finishes their piece and joins the other artists, all now present together in the Gallery. This gathering of all artists in one place is the clearest signal of the day that something has shifted.

- Aspettatori approach with the barter item they held back specifically for this.
- Pieces are discussed. Sometimes two aspettatori offer different objects for the same piece — the first real moment of scarcity and choice in the day.
- The artist (as **Producer** of their own piece) decides. This authority is real, and specific to the piece they made — the one place in the structure where a performer exercises deliberate judgement over aspettatori.
- No one leaves empty-handed by design: an unsuccessful barter simply redirects — to another piece, or to another aspettatore, later in the day.

---

## Act 3 — The Lavagna / Inclusivity Layer

Act 3 is not a separate register so much as the day's escape valve: for anyone without the right object in hand, or for anything too large to carry — a favour, a skill, lodging — the blackboard lets the exchange still happen.

- The artist may choose to accept a promise instead of a bartered object, in place of (not alongside) the Gallery-floor negotiation.
- The **Remembrancer** — not the Producer, not the aspettatore — chalks each promise onto the board, under the heading **OFFERTE E FAVORI**.
- The board can also make public other exchanges agreed to occur physically at the next rehearsal — commitments unconnected to today's barters.
- No completion timeframe is set for any promise. Trust carries across time the same way it carries across the afternoon.
- The Remembrancer decides when Act 3 is finished — their call, no fixed timeframe — then rings a bell, takes a photo of the board, and wipes it clean. Every promise has been recorded; nothing physically persists on-site.
  - The bell is the day's second and last deliberate audible signal (the toast, below, being the first and only spoken line) — everything else in the day has been silent and physical.
  - The photograph feeds the running archive (BosaNode); the Remembrancer's once-a-year bound volume is a separate, hand-copied artifact drawing on the year's rehearsals, not a live transcription of the board itself.
  - Because the board is wiped, the photograph is the only surviving record of what's still outstanding — there is no live, cumulative "open promises" list unless someone deliberately builds one from the photo archive.

---

## Il Brindisi

- Triggered by the Remembrancer's bell — the day's clearest, and only audible, cue.
- A small bar: one chiller tap, one size of glass. The barman fills each glass as people gather.
- For rehearsals, the tap pours cold water, not beer — this keeps the toast open to everyone present, children included.
- Once everyone holds a filled glass, the barman offers the toast — the only performed vocal line in the entire day: *"Saludu e Trigu. A kent'annos."* The crowd repeats it back.
- For the adults, the barman then passes among the crowd offering to top up their glass with wine — local red, or malvasia spumante on special days.
- That is the end of the performance. No further signal, no formal curtain.
- The drinks ceremony fully dissolves the line between performers and spectators.
- A performer may leave unnoticed — closure belongs to whoever stays, not an obligation on whoever leaves.
- "La performance finisce dove è iniziata. La Prova è una prova."

## Backstage — Booking & Manifest

*This section exists purely to make the day run smoothly. None of it is visible to, or changes anything about, the on-site experience for eaters, aspettatori, or artists — everything above this line still happens with no announcement, no hurry, no coordination. This is what makes that possible.*

The day should feel like nothing was planned. Achieving that requires real planning behind it — people, place, and props, decided in advance so the truck can be loaded and the site can hold whatever turns up.

**What gets booked (capacity-limited, physical)**
- Mats (L'Usato) — self-booked, no approval step
- Pedestals/easels (the Gallery) — self-booked; the first four reserved for artists, in their agreed slot order
- Workbench slots (Trasformazione) — booked in advance, in order, per artist
- Parking, if the site has a hard limit

**What stays uncounted (elastic by nature)**
- Lo Spuntino's shared table — always open, regardless of numbers
- Aspettatori themselves — no cap, no registration
- Barter and gift items brought on the day

**Booking mechanics**
- First-come, first-served. No gatekeeping, no approval.
- A booking is a number, not a commitment — a no-show costs nothing and needs no follow-up, consistent with the rest of the day's ethos.
- Someone who hasn't booked can still show up and take a mat or pedestal if slack remains once the manifest is loaded.
- Cutoff for each rehearsal is announced in the WhatsApp group ahead of time; bookings lock at that point so the manifest can be finalised.

**The manifest**
- The truck's actual load-out for a given rehearsal, derived from that rehearsal's booking counts at cutoff: N mats, N pedestals, 1 workbench (or more, at scale), plus fixed base kit — Lo Spuntino's table, the blackboard and chalk, whatever is always loaded regardless of numbers.
- Scales up or down freely rehearsal to rehearsal. Nothing about the performance's structure changes at any scale — only the count of mats and pedestals.
