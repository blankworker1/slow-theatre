# Il Commerciante

*A satellite role of Slow Theatre / La Prova. A separate layer, running alongside Act One without altering it — present on some days, absent on others, never required for the performance to complete.*

---

## I. Historical context

Every clearance table has always drawn a particular kind of visitor. Long before eBay, before lock-up storage units and car-boot sales, there was the picker — the *rigattiere*, the *brocanteur*, the dealer in used and unwanted things — who walked the estate sale, the house clearance, the market stall, with a different eye than everyone else. Not looking for something to use, not looking for something to make. Looking for something to move. What passed through their hands rarely stayed still for long: bought for little from someone who no longer wanted it, sold on for more to someone who would.

This figure has never needed introducing. Every town has known one, by name or by reputation — the person you call when a house needs clearing, who takes what's left after the family has taken what it wants, who turns over what nobody else saw a use for. The trade is centuries old, entirely ordinary, and entirely legitimate: it is commerce in its plainest form, buying low and selling on, done in the open, taxed and accounted for like any other business. Il Commerciante is not a new invention. It is that same, already-familiar figure, simply present at the table.

## II. The performance layer

Il Commerciante is a filter, not a performer. What passes through their hands at the table either leaves changed, or leaves recognized — nothing passes through unaltered by their attention. Some objects need work: cleaned, repaired, restored to a state someone will pay for. Others need nothing physical at all — only the eye to recognize what an object is actually worth, and the network and know-how to realize that value somewhere it can be realized. Two different skills, the same function: converting what the table calls rubbish into what a market will call a product.

Nothing about this needs staging. Il Commerciante is distinguished from anyone else at the table only by how they look at what's there — faster, more assessing, sometimes a phone out for a moment to check a price elsewhere — and by what happens after they leave, somewhere off the stage entirely, where the cleaning, the repair, or the sale takes place. Nobody announces them, and nobody needs to. This role asks nothing of whoever fills it beyond being themselves: a real secondhand dealer, restorer, or trader could occupy it exactly as they already are, doing what they'd do at any clearance sale in any town, without being asked to perform anything beyond their ordinary practice. In this it follows the same pattern the Watcher's role revealed — some parts of the performance don't need casting, because the world already supplies someone who fills them without being invited.

## III. What it completes

Without Il Commerciante, Act One demonstrates three lawful categories sitting side by side: donazione (the gift, no expectation of return), permuta (barter, value for value), and the occasional private resale of one's own used things (lawful, one-off, outside the abitualità threshold). All three are informal, personal, and effectively invisible to the state — not because they're hidden, but because none of them are the state's business.

Il Commerciante adds a fourth category, and it is the different one: genuine, habitual, professional trade — buying with the intention of resale, repeatedly, as an occupation. This is exactly the activity Italian law expects to see registered, taxed, and accounted for, whether under partita IVA or regime forfettario. Their presence doesn't undermine the demonstration — it completes it honestly. The performance isn't claiming that all commerce escapes the state's reach. It's showing precisely where the boundary actually sits: everything on one side of Il Commerciante needs no permission and no ledger; everything on their side of it always has, and always should.

This is also why Il Commerciante stands outside La Prova in a way none of the other roles do. Il Proprietario, l'Ospite, la Comunità, il Testimone — none of them exist as legal persons; they're roles anyone present can step into and out of, with no registration behind any of them, which is precisely the point of Act One and Act Two. Il Commerciante is the opposite: a real legal and fiscal actor, with standing that exists whether or not La Prova is happening at all. They don't step into a role invented by the performance — they simply continue being who they already are, in front of it. The filter operates on the object; it does not touch the performance, and the performance does not touch it back.

Whether something Il Commerciante carries away ever resurfaces — reframed, restaged, priced, in a shop window or an Act Two elsewhere — is not for the performance to say. That possibility is left open, the same way most things here are: visible enough to imagine, never confirmed.

## IV. Requirements

No rehearsal. No induction. No agreement to appear on any given day. The role exists whenever someone already doing this for a living happens to pass through the square and does what they always do — and it simply continues not existing on the days they don't. Il Commerciante is never invited, never paid an appearance fee, never thanked publicly, and never listed in anything handed to a comune. They are not a performer in the sense the rest of the cast are. They are the ordinary economy, walking through, exactly as it already does.
