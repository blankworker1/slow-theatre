# La Sarta

*A satellite role of Slow Theatre / La Prova. An autonomous mini-stage running alongside Act One and Act Two, present in its own right and never folded into the main pedestal's choreography.*

---

## I. Historical context

Before ready-made clothing, there was mending, remaking, and making do — a trade practiced at home or in a small shop, on a machine driven by nothing but a foot or a hand. The treadle sewing machine belongs to that world: no motor hiding the mechanism, the work visibly done at the speed a body can move it, the same machine that clothed a household for a generation before anyone thought to call that slow. It is loud, rhythmic, and entirely legible — anyone watching can see the needle catch the thread, the wheel turn, the seam form, with nothing concealed inside a casing.

Every svotta cantina in Sardinia turns up the same thing: boxes of old linens, half-finished fabric, clothes nobody will wear again. This was never a supply problem waiting to be solved. It's a supply already sitting in people's houses, waiting only for a reason to leave — the same raw material Act One already exists to receive, worked instead of simply given away.

## II. The performance layer

La Sarta works two acts, and only two — there is no Act Three function, no lavagna, no promise to record.

**Act One.** Autonomous work at the treadle machine: quick repairs to something someone's wearing, or slower remaking of donated fabric into new small things — a bag, a patch, a child's garment — worked live, at her own pace, on her own station. This closes a loop none of the other satellite crafts quite reach: rather than adding a maker's time to raw material, La Sarta takes something someone already owns and no longer wants and turns it into something they, or someone else, does want — barter in miniature, worn cloth in, remade object out, with no money anywhere in the transaction.

**Act Two.** The everyday work continues on its own mini-stage — a self-contained loop, running the whole time Act Two is live, entirely separate from the pedestal's main exchange. People drift over on their own schedule; nothing cues them there, and nothing about the mini-stage competes with the pedestal for attention. Occasionally, though, La Sarta brings a specially made garment, finished ahead of time and displayed on a tailor's dummy, which graduates onto the pedestal as an art piece in its own right — legible the same way anything else there is legible, no explanation needed.

## III. What it completes

The pedestal is the main stage — used objects, offered and exchanged, one clear place where the ritual of Act Two actually happens. If every satellite fed into that ritual, the pedestal would stop being the one thing people need to watch, and the whole act would start to resemble a row of stalls all doing the same thing, which is precisely the craft-fair reading Slow Theatre exists to avoid. La Sarta instead does something different, at her own pace, mostly at arm's length from the main exchange — and only occasionally, deliberately, sends a single finished piece across that distance onto the pedestal itself.

That single rule — a satellite stays autonomous, but may graduate one object at a time into the main stage — is what separates La Sarta from a craft demonstration. Each garment she makes is unique by construction; a unique object is an artwork by definition, the same test every other satellite role in the project is held to. Nothing about her presence needs justifying beyond that: the growing happens at her machine, unhurried; the eating — the offering and exchange — happens only at the pedestal, and only when she chooses to send something there.

## IV. Requirements

A treadle or hand-crank machine, and nothing else that isn't already sitting in a svotta cantina somewhere. No supply table needs building in advance — fabric arrives the same way every other object in Act One does, through the same table, the same clear-outs. No rehearsal beyond the craft itself, no cue to wait for, no fixed brief for what gets made or when a piece is ready to graduate. La Sarta decides that on the day, the same judgment every other satellite role in Slow Theatre is trusted to make for itself.
