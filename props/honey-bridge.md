# Honey Bridge

*A one-time, documented trial — not a standing practice, not a new
mechanic, and never to be repeated without the same open planning and
honest recording described here.*

## The idea, briefly

Economists call it a bridge good — something accepted in a trade not
purely for its own sake, but partly because it's widely, genuinely wanted
and therefore easy to trade onward. Historically, this is proto-money:
cattle, salt, tobacco in closed economies, the earliest step toward
currency. That's exactly why it's approached carefully here rather than
built into the performance outright — see the reasoning already laid out
in bosataz-fiscal.md and understanding-permuta.md. A designated,
recurring bridge good would risk becoming exactly the kind of
"stabile nel tempo, ripetuta con organizzazione" pattern abitualità
exists to catch, and would quietly reintroduce the instrumental thinking
Currency and Character argues the whole project exists to remove.

The safeguard isn't secrecy. It's scope, honesty, and a defined stop.

## What's actually being tried

At one specific rehearsal, agreed in advance among whoever's running it
— not announced to the crowd, the same unexplained texture as everything
else in the physical layer — small, unbranded jars of honey (roughly
500g, plainly sealed, no label beyond what's needed to identify the
contents) enter the day in two ways:

- **In L'Usato**, gifted to early participants. No strings, no
  expectation — ordinary Act One giving, just seeded with something
  chosen because it's genuinely, widely liked.
- **In L'Arte**, offered by whoever's running the trial as their own
  trade for a piece of art, when the offer is honest — something they'd
  genuinely be glad to receive that piece for, with the honey simply
  making the trade an easy, welcome one to accept, not a trick to acquire
  work cheaply.

## What this is testing

Two different questions, worth keeping separate:

1. **In Act One** — does a genuinely likeable, freely given good actually
   get re-offered downstream by whoever received it? Real Donatore
   behaviour, just given a nudge.
2. **In Act Two** — does honey function as a bridge good in a real trade —
   accepted readily not because the other party was craving honey in that
   exact moment, but because it's a convenient, known-tradeable thing
   alongside genuine appreciation for the exchange?

## The boundaries that make this honest

- **Unbranded, plainly sealed.** Legible only as "honey," never as a
  product or a presence — the same isolation-is-the-signal grammar
  already used for everything on a plinth.
- **One rehearsal, defined in advance, with a defined stop.** Not an
  ongoing practice. Doing this again would need the same open agreement
  and honest recording as the first time — never a quiet continuation.
- **Openly planned, not openly announced.** Nothing said to the crowd in
  the moment — but genuinely agreed beforehand among the people actually
  running that day, so nobody involved is acting alone or unknowingly.
- **Recorded honestly afterward.** The Remembrancer's chronicle page for
  that rehearsal notes what was tried and what actually happened, in the
  same register as any other page — an observation, not a hidden
  intervention. This is what separates the trial from anything closer to
  "laundering": not that it's quiet in the moment, but that there's a
  true account of it afterward.

## What this isn't

Not an attempt to make honey a de facto currency. Not a mechanic to be
built into the script, the staging document, or any future rehearsal by
default. If honey — or anything else — genuinely turns out to trade
unusually easily across several *unseeded* rehearsals, that's a pattern
worth the Remembrancer noticing and writing down on its own merits. This
document only covers the one deliberate, bounded trial described above.

## After the seeding: what the Remembrancer watches for

The trial itself is one bounded event. What follows it isn't a second
trial — it's ordinary noticing, folded into the same page-by-page
chronicle the Remembrancer already keeps. From the rehearsal after the
seeding onward, honey's presence — or absence — simply becomes one more
thing worth a line in the day's account: does anyone bring their own
honey to give, unprompted; does anything that looks like one of the
original jars turn up again, offered or given by someone else; does
honey stop appearing at all.

Worth being honest that the original jars are, by design, unbranded and
therefore unidentifiable — there's no way to confirm a jar seen later
actually is one of the seeded ones rather than honey someone brought on
their own. That ambiguity isn't a flaw to fix; a marked or tracked jar
would turn an honest gift into a tracked instrument, the exact thing the
trial was built to avoid. The seeding was a single, deliberate act. The
recurrence — if there is any — is simply what happens next, watched for
the same patient, unhurried way the Remembrancer watches everything
else, with no expectation of a particular answer either way.
