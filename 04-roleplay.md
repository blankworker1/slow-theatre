# Roleplay in Slow Theatre

*What we mean by "acting" — and what we don't*

---

## Traditional acting

The version most people picture first: learn the lines, wear the costume, become someone else for the duration of the show. The actor and the character are two separate things — Hamlet is not the person playing him. A skill is being performed: memory, voice, physicality, convincing pretence. When the show ends, the costume comes off and the character is set down like a coat.

This is the version that makes people nervous. *I'm not an actor. I'd freeze. I don't know how to "become" someone else.* It's also the version Slow Theatre has almost nothing to do with.

## Method acting

A different tradition: instead of pretending to feel something, the actor finds a real feeling in themselves and lets it inform the role. Real grief is used to play grief. The self becomes raw material for the fiction, rather than staying separate from it. The line between actor and character gets deliberately blurred — sometimes for the length of a whole production, staying "in character" between takes.

But even here, there's still a fiction being served, and it still ends. However far the actor lets the role in, there's a wrap, a cut, a moment where they step back out. The character was always borrowed, never kept.

## Reality TV

A third model, and closer to what we're doing — but not quite it. In reality TV there's no character at all. People play *themselves*, using their real name and real reasoning, inside a situation that's been deliberately constructed — a house, a competition, a set of rules. They know the premise is artificial. Their reactions inside it are not. What you see is a real person's real behaviour, shaped by a constructed frame rather than a script.

What reality TV gets right: no pretending, no becoming someone else, nothing to rehearse but your own honesty. What it gets wrong, for our purposes: **it never ends.** The cameras keep rolling. The format doesn't release its participants — they stay inside the premise for weeks, and there's no shared, visible moment where everyone agrees it's over and steps back into ordinary life together.

## How it works in Slow Theatre

Slow Theatre takes reality TV's honesty and gives it back traditional theatre's release.

**You are not playing a character.** There are no lines to learn and nothing to become. You bring your actual name, your actual reasoning, your actual reluctance or enthusiasm. If you're exchanging something, it's really yours and you really want to part with it or you don't. The roles — seller, witness, artist — are frames placed around a real person, not masks replacing one.

**The premise is constructed on purpose, and that's fine to know.** Unlike traditional acting, there's nothing to convincingly perform. Unlike invisible theatre (which some parts of la prova borrow from for outside observers), participants themselves are let in on the frame — you know a situation has been set up, the same way a reality show's cast knows the house is a set. What you do inside it is still genuinely yours.

**There's no opening night.** No clapperboard, no announcement, no moment where anyone says "begin." People arrive at slightly different times. Someone starts laying out the table, someone else pulls up, a passerby slows down to look. There's no fixed point where the show starts — and yet everyone there somehow already knows that it has. Nobody needs to be told; the setup itself is already inside the performance, not a preamble to it.

**And then: the toast.** This is where Slow Theatre parts ways with reality TV entirely. When the truck packs up and everyone shares a drink together, the performance is over. Not gradually, not ambiguously — visibly and together, in front of each other. This isn't a curtain falling cleanly: it's a softer closure, giving the prova what it needs without declaring it finished with a clean cut. But the signal still stands — the premise closes, and whoever's there simply becomes themselves again. Nothing carries over. Nothing follows anyone home.

This closure is what makes everything before it safe to attempt. It's also what makes recurrence possible: because nothing lingers from one prova to the next, each one can start clean, rather than dragging residue forward the way rivalries and alliances accumulate across an entire reality TV season.

---

**In short:**

- Not traditional acting — nothing to learn, no character to become.
- Not quite method acting — no fiction being served, nothing to access on someone else's behalf.
- Closest to reality TV — real self, constructed premise, unscripted reactions.
- But with the one thing reality TV refuses to give: a shared, visible closure, after which the performance releases everyone completely.

*You don't need to know how to act. You need to know when the toast starts.*
