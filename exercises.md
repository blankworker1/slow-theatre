# Exercises

*A growing companion to the philosophical documents — not explanations
of the structure, but small, optional, bounded rehearsals of one
specific reflex at a time, practiced before the stakes are real. Il
lupo perde il pelo ma non il vizio names the vice and tells you to
let the impulse pass. An exercise is the thing you actually do to
build the muscle that instruction describes.*

## RESERVATO

**The convention.** A small sticker, placed on an object, reading
simply RESERVATO. No sign explains it, no performer announces it,
and none of that is needed — everyone already carries the same
reflex from ordinary life. A restaurant table marked *riservato*,
empty and visibly available, still gets left alone. Nobody flags
down the waiter to argue. Nobody asks why. The marker alone does
all the work a lock or a bouncer would otherwise have to do. This
exercise imports that exact convention, unchanged, into Act One.

**Two lessons, one sticker.**

*For the Donatore, it's agency without justification.* This
agency already exists in Act One's own rule — the giver decides,
privately, full stop — it just usually stays invisible: someone
simply doesn't put an object out, or passes it to a friend quietly,
and no one else is any the wiser. RESERVATO doesn't grant new
power. It makes existing, private discretion visible for a moment,
without converting it into something that then has to be defended
because it's now public. That's the genuinely useful part: making a
boundary visible usually invites scrutiny of it. This convention is
one of the rare cases where visibility and zero justification
coexist without friction.

*For the aspetta-tore, it's the harder half.* Wanting something,
seeing it sitting right there, unclaimed, reachable — and simply
not reaching, not asking why, not turning someone else's private
choice into a problem for them to solve. Where the money-vice is a
compulsion to price things, this is a compulsion to feel owed an
explanation for someone else's line. The exercise is the same
shape as the wolf/vice document's core instruction, aimed at a
different reflex: not "don't reach for a price," but "don't reach
for a reason."

**A third, practical use, requiring no separate convention.** The
same sticker, same expected response, also protects whatever's
present at a rehearsal but was never part of the gift table at
all — tools, equipment, the truck's own kit, anything sharing the
same open, unguarded space as L'Usato's objects without actually
being offered. An aspetta-tore trained on the first meaning behaves
correctly around the second without ever being told there's a
difference. The lesson was never "understand why a thing is
reserved." It was "don't need to" — which turns out to cover both
cases with the same sticker, the same silence, and the same
respected boundary.

**Using it well.** RESERVATO stays an occasional, deliberate choice,
not a default. Act One's anonymity — nobody chosen, nobody
unchosen, to take part — is what keeps its threshold as low as it
is; if reservation became the norm rather than the exception, Act
One would start quietly importing Act Three's selectivity without
Act Three's safeguard (a promise is witnessed and accountable
precisely because it's directed at someone by name; a reserved
object, used too often, would be just as directed with none of
that accountability behind it). Used sparingly, on purpose, it
stays a rehearsal tool. Used constantly, it would quietly become a
different table.
