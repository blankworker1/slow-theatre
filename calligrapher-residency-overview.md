# A calligrapher in Bosa — an idea for the residency

## The short version

I'd love it if one of your upcoming resident artists was a calligrapher —
someone who works with historical or manuscript scripts, not just modern
lettering — coming to Bosa to make calligraphic artwork. That's genuinely
the whole ask. You'd have another good resident, they'd have a beautiful
place to work, and that's a complete outcome on its own, regardless of
anything else in this document.

Everything below is just me explaining why I care about this particular
kind of resident, in case it's useful context — not a condition attached
to the idea.

## The background

For a while now I've been building something called **Slow Theatre** — a
street performance project, currently centred on Bosa, built around a
recurring piece called **La Prova**. It runs entirely on gift and barter,
no money changing hands: someone donates something they no longer want,
it either gets transformed into art, repaired and resold, or genuinely
recycled, and the whole thing closes with an open pledge written on a
blackboard and a shared drink.

One role in this whole structure is called the **Remembrancer** — the
person who records that a performance happened at all: a dated log, a
photo of that day's blackboard, a line in their own hand. It's meant to
be a real craft role, not an administrative one — the record itself
treated as worth making beautifully, not just accurately.

I'd been thinking about that role through the lens of the old European
guild system — apprentice, journeyman, master — and specifically through
something real and still-practiced: the **Wanderjahre**, where a
journeyman craftsman spends years travelling between towns and masters
before settling. It's not currently a tradition calligraphy takes part
in, historically it was (painters, goldsmiths, and mason-architects did
take these journeys in the medieval and Renaissance guild period) — so
reviving it for calligraphy specifically would be new, not a rediscovery.

The piece that made this click for me: a **masterpiece**, in the old
guild sense, without any panel or gatekeeper — a Remembrancer becomes a
master the moment they've completed a substantial enough piece of work,
judged by nobody but whoever sees it. For Bosa specifically, the obvious
candidate is a full hand-copied transcription of the **Carta de Logu** —
Eleonora d'Arborea's 14th-century legal code, written in Sardinian
itself, 198 chapters, the law of Sardinia for nearly 450 years. It's
hard to think of a more fitting subject for someone to spend real time
with, here.

## Why the residency call itself should stay simple

None of this needs to be in the brief you post, and I'd actively rather
it wasn't. The right person for this would be someone who finds
manuscript work and old scripts genuinely interesting on their own
terms — not someone applying because a mythology sounded compelling.
"Come make calligraphic artworks in Bosa" is the whole call. If someone
turns out to be the right kind of curious once they're actually here,
that conversation happens naturally, face to face, not as a pitch.

## What I'm hoping for, loosely

If the resident seems interested once they've settled in, I'd love to
show them Bosa's version of La Prova properly — maybe even invite them
to sit in on one rehearsal, purely to see what it is, no expectation
attached. Whether anything comes of that is genuinely up to them and
what they respond to. If nothing does, you've still had a good
calligrapher in residence, which was always reason enough on its own.

## What I'd actually ask of you

Just this: when you're looking at applicants, if two people are
otherwise equally strong, lean toward whoever shows real interest in
historical or manuscript scripts over someone doing purely modern or
decorative lettering. That's the only thing that would make this more
likely to go somewhere interesting — everything else is just me thinking
out loud.
