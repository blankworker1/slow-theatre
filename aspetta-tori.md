# Gli Aspetta-tori

*The lowest-threshold role in Slow Theatre. Not a role anyone takes on — the state everyone is already in, before anything else happens.*

---

## I. Historical context

Boal gave the world *spett-attore* — spectator and actor fused into one word, activated the moment someone in the audience is called into the scene. It was a real breakthrough: theatre that refused to let its audience stay passive, that insisted watching and doing were never as separate as the proscenium arch pretended. But Boal's fusion still depends on a caller. Someone — the Joker, the facilitator, the structure of the exercise itself — has to reach into the audience and pull a person across the line. The boundary is real until someone with standing to do so breaks it.

*Aspetta-tori* starts from a different premise, and Italian hands it over almost for free: *spettatori* (spectators) and *aspettare* (to wait) sit close enough in the language that the pun barely needs inventing. It names not a fusion but a state — the condition of standing at the table, or at the edge of the piazza, watching, in a waiting that is not empty but loaded, already coiled toward becoming action the moment someone decides to move. Nobody calls anyone into it, because in this project there is nowhere to call from. *Nessuno chiama perché non c'è da dove chiamare.* No Joker, no facilitator, no gatekeeper holding the invitation. The audience was never on the other side of a line to begin with — only unentered.

## II. The performance layer

Aspetta-tori requires nothing: no signup, no costume, no cue, no acknowledgment from anyone else present. It is simply what a person is doing the moment they arrive and start watching — at the table in Act One, at the pedestal in Act Two, in the piazza during Act Three. Nothing distinguishes an aspetta-tore from anyone else nearby, because there is nothing to distinguish. This is the zero-cost entry point for the entire performance: presence, unannounced, unrequested, sufficient in itself.

Every other role in the project — Ospite, Comunità, Testimone, even a satellite figure like Il Commerciante — is really just aspetta-tori that has, for a moment, moved. Someone standing at the table deciding whether to take something, someone watching the pedestal decide whether to bid, someone in the crowd during Act Three deciding whether to speak into the microphone: all of them were aspetta-tori first, and become something else only by their own motion, never by invitation. The naming is not a footnote describing the audience. It is already part of the performance — calling them aspetta-tori is itself the permission, granted in advance, requiring no one to hand it out on the day.

## III. What it completes

Every other role in the project has some threshold, however low — walking up to the table, picking something up, stepping toward the pedestal, taking the microphone. Aspetta-tori has none. It is the floor beneath all the other thresholds, the state a person is already in before they cross any of them, and the state they return to the moment they stop. Without it named, the project would still work, but it would leave an unexamined assumption sitting underneath everything else: that the crowd is just backdrop, waiting to be activated by someone else's cue. Naming it closes that gap. There is no backdrop. There is only aspetta-tori, some of it in motion and most of it not, and the performance never asks which is which.

## IV. Requirements

None. This is the only role in the project that cannot be failed, refused, or withheld — anyone present already holds it, by default, without asking and without being asked.
