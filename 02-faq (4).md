# Slow Theatre — Frequently Asked Questions

*A companion to the Slow Theatre philosophy document*

---

**What is Slow Food?**

Slow Food is the international movement Slow Theatre draws its philosophy from — food that's Good, Clean, and Fair: good in taste and quality, clean for the environment it comes from, fair to the people who grow and make it. Slow Theatre takes that same three-part standard and applies it to performance instead of food.

**What is BosaNode?**

BosaNode is the name for the Slow Theatre community based in Bosa — the people bringing this philosophy to life locally, starting with La Prova.

**What is La Prova?**

La Prova is the first performance built on Slow Theatre's philosophy — a four-act street performance, staged from the back of a truck, built around gift, barter, and public promises rather than tickets or a script to memorise. Performed in Bosa, by BosaNode.

**What is BosaTAZ?**

BosaTAZ is a planned future digital layer for La Prova — a small, offline network the truck would eventually carry, letting anyone nearby find out more at their own pace, with nothing explained to them out loud. It doesn't exist yet; this describes what's planned, not something currently running.

**Who can participate?**

Anyone present. Slow Theatre doesn't separate performers from audience — the line is left deliberately open, so that watching and acting can flow into each other without anyone asking permission first. There's no membership, no audition, no application. You take part by showing up.

**Do I need any experience or training?**

No. Slow Theatre is built on presence and willingness, not technique. What matters is being genuinely there — bringing something real from the place and the moment — not performing skill.

**When does it happen?**

There's no season and no premiere. Slow Theatre works through recurrence: the same kind of gathering happens again and again, each time treated as one more rehearsal rather than a final version. Nothing has to be resolved or finished in a single sitting — a piece can be picked up again next time it happens. Watch for announced dates and places; there's no fixed calendar to follow, because the pace is set by the community, not a schedule.

**Where does it take place?**

Wherever the local material actually is — a piazza, a street, a room where people already gather. The setting is chosen to fit what's local, not the other way around.

**Who covers the costs?**

Nobody is sponsoring this, and that's intentional — Slow Theatre stays "clean" by having no outside funder or commercial backer to answer to. Each participant carries their own part: their own time, their own materials, their own risk. There's no ticket price and no fee to take part. The lack of a big budget is a feature, not a limitation — it keeps the work honest and keeps the decisions in the room, not with whoever is paying.

**Is there an organiser in charge?**

Someone usually sets a date, picks a place, and shows up to get things started — but that role is practical, not authoritative. There's no director, no committee, no one with final say over what the work becomes. Decisions belong to whoever is present.

**Who is the producer/director?**

Nobody, and this isn't an oversight — the role has nothing to attach to. Directing only makes sense for someone shaping a character: you can coach how a role behaves, not how a real person actually chooses to give something away or make a promise to a stranger. Slow Theatre has no characters to direct, only people being themselves, so there's simply no job for a director to do. What does the shaping instead is the protocol itself — the acts, the roles, the constraints — written once, fixed, and read the same by everyone, doing the work a director's ongoing judgment would otherwise do.

**Do I need to bring anything?**

Only what feels honest to bring — an object, a story, a question, or simply yourself. There's no required contribution and no wrong way to arrive.

**Is this the same every time?**

No. Each gathering is its own instance of the same underlying philosophy — Good, Clean, and Fair, grown patiently and locally — but the specific shape it takes (what's staged, who's there, what happens) is different every time. That variation is part of the point.

**What if I just want to watch?**

That's welcome too. Nobody is required to step forward. But watching here is understood as a kind of waiting that can turn into action at any moment — not a fixed, separate role.

**How is this different from a normal show?**

A normal show is finished, ticketed, and built to be reproduced the same way in any town. Slow Theatre is unfinished by design, free to take part in, and shaped entirely by whoever and whatever is local to that place and that day.

**Can I bring this idea somewhere else — start my own version?**

Yes. Slow Theatre is offered as a philosophy and a starting point, not a fixed format or a brand. If the same three principles are honoured — Good, Clean, Fair — it's already Slow Theatre, wherever it happens.
