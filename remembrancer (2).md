# The Remembrancer

*A role of Slow Theatre. First among the satellite roles, and the one through which the work outlives its own performances.*

---

## I. Historical context

Before there were archives, there were hands. Long before ledgers, cameras, or clouds, communities kept their own history the same way: someone sat, and wrote, and what they wrote lasted. A parish register, a ship's log, an illuminated chapter — these were never neutral instruments. They were written in the voice of whoever held the pen, on a given day, in a given light, and that voice is still legible centuries later without anything standing between the page and the reader. No file format to become obsolete. No account to expire. No company that can revoke it.

The Remembrancer returns to this method deliberately, not out of nostalgia but because it is still, today, the most sustainable form of record that exists. It asks nothing of the future except that someone can read. It requires no power, no platform, no maintenance. It is human-scale in the most literal sense: the record is made at the speed a hand can move, and it will still be legible at the speed a hand can move a hundred years from now.

This is the Slow ethos applied not to food, or exchange, or time itself, but to memory. If Slow Theatre insists that nothing here should be rushed toward a fixed and final meaning, the Remembrancer is what allows that patience to be witnessed at all — because without a record, "slow" leaves no trace of having passed.

Bosa itself already holds the deepest precedent for this. The Carta de Logu, promulgated by Eleonora d'Arborea at the end of the fourteenth century, governed Sardinian life for over four centuries — a legal code written locally, in the Sardinian tongue, and simply kept and obeyed, without needing an empire or a capital to enforce it. It is proof that a hand-written, place-specific record can carry real, lasting authority purely on the strength of being trusted and preserved. The Remembrancer's work is not legal, but it inherits the same logic: a record made in a place, for that place, becomes part of how the place understands its own continuity.

## II. The performance layer

The Remembrancer is a layer of La Prova, in the same sense as the other overlaid presences the project allows: something that shares the same place and the same constraints, without ever announcing itself, without ever interrupting the acts already underway.

**What the role is not.** The Remembrancer is not a narrator, not a host, not an explainer. They do not introduce La Prova, describe it, or perform toward the audience. They do not take part in exchange — nothing they hold is offered at the pedestal, nothing they do belongs to Atto Due's economy of value-for-value. They are present the way the market table or the pedestal is present: because it is simply there, doing what it does, needing no permission and no announcement.

**Where they sit across the acts.**
- **L'Usato (Act One):** works autonomously, at the edge of the table, recording what arrives, unhurried, as objects find the stall. This is also where the Carta de Logu dissemination task (Section III) takes physical place, week by week.
- **L'Arte (Act Two):** may display a single illuminated page as an artwork in its own right, standing alongside the pedestals — the only object in Act Two that is displayed but was never intended for exchange. On weeks when a Carta de Logu page has just been completed, this is the page shown.
- **La Promessa (Act Three):** becomes the hand at the lavagna — the one who writes the day's OFFERTE E FAVORI in chalk, provisional and legible, exactly as the board convention already requires.

**How they behave.** Silent and fixed, in the terms La Prova already uses for its layers: no scripted lines, no interaction beyond the writing itself, no variation in manner from one rehearsal to the next. Consistency, not performance, is what makes the presence legible over time.

**What they produce.** One page per rehearsal — a record of what happened, written by hand, in the voice of the writer. Not a transcript, not an inventory: an account, the way a log or a chronicle is an account, shaped by what the writer noticed and chose to set down. The pages accumulate loose through the year.

## III. The long task: disseminating the Carta de Logu

Alongside the chronicle pages, the Remembrancer carries a second, much slower undertaking: a complete hand-copied transcription of the Carta de Logu, the oldest surviving Sardinian legal text and the foundational document of Bosa's own deep history — produced one chapter, one page, at a time, one per rehearsal.

**Why this exists.** The chronicle records La Prova. The Carta de Logu copying disseminates something older and, to most people, entirely unknown: a 630-year-old law written in Sardinian for Sardinians, largely unread outside specialist study. Each week's page is genuinely new to almost everyone who sees it — not old news dressed as new, but honest first contact with a text that has simply never reached most of the people standing in front of it.

**Form.** Plain black ink, in the historically accurate register established by the surviving manuscript and early printed editions: continuous calligraphic hand, no illustrative imagery, one modest illuminated capital at the opening of each chapter and nothing more. The Sardinian original is the primary text; a translation (Italian, or others as needed) may accompany it, facing or beneath, echoing the bilingual convention of the historical printed editions rather than inventing a new format.

**Pace and scale.** The original Carta de Logu, as promulgated by Eleonora d'Arborea before later editors appended her father's rural code, runs to 162 chapters across 96 manuscript pages. At one page per rehearsal, a complete copy takes roughly 96 rehearsals — several years, depending on how often La Prova runs. This is deliberate, not a limitation to be solved. The task is long enough that "when will it be finished" has one honest answer: when the book is complete. In practice, that keeps the horizon far enough away that the practice itself remains the point, the same way none of La Prova's other layers resolve or explain themselves.

**Chapter order and content.** Chapters are copied in their original sequence, without curation or selection by the Remembrancer — the variety visitors encounter week to week (theft and punishment, livestock and branding disputes, testimony and proof, sacred property, holy days, land and inheritance) is inherent to the source text, not authored. The Remembrancer's own editorial discretion stays confined to the chronicle pages; the Carta de Logu pages are purely disseminatory, page following page in Eleonora's own order.

**Relationship to the chronicle.** The two undertakings run on different clocks and are never confused with one another. The chronicle is personal, interpretive, and resolves annually at binding. The Carta de Logu copy is impersonal, sequential, and resolves only once, after however many years it takes — a second, slower rhythm layered on top of the yearly one.

**What happens to the pages.** As with the chronicle, individual Carta de Logu pages may be shown in Act Two as they are completed, but are not exchanged. Whether completed Carta de Logu pages are eventually bound as their own separate volume, folded into the annual chronicle binding, or handled some other way is not yet decided, and does not need to be decided now.

## IV. The community wall record

Once a year, the loose pages of a completed chronicle cycle are bound. The binding is its own small rite — the one moment in the whole project that is allowed to look like a formal ceremony, because it marks the one object in the system that is not meant to keep changing state.

The bound volume is handed from the Remembrancer, its guardian while it was still being made, to the town itself. It is placed in a small wall niche in Bosa — the same gesture, the same scale, as a wayside Madonna set into a village wall. It is displayed there permanently. It is never exchanged, never removed for barter, never returned to circulation. Where every other object in La Prova is free to move between states — art, gift, rubbish, useful thing, art again — the bound volume is the single fixed point. It is looked at, not used.

Over years, each niche holds one volume, one cycle, dated and never reopened. Together they form a walkable record through Bosa itself: not an archive kept behind a door, but a trail anyone can pass on an ordinary street, needing no explanation because the form — something precious, kept behind glass, in a wall — already carries its own meaning.

## V. Role requirements

**Essential**
- Comfortable and genuinely skilled at handwriting as a craft — legible, considered, and consistent in hand across a full year of entries
- Able to attend rehearsals regularly, over months and years, without needing the day announced or explained in advance
- Willing to work in silence, without interacting, narrating, or drawing attention to the act of recording
- Discretion: writes what they themselves witness and judge worth setting down, without needing a fixed brief
- Able to read and transcribe Sardinian (Arborense/Logudorese) source text accurately, letter by letter, even without full fluency

**Valued, not mandatory**
- Experience in calligraphy, illumination, or manuscript illustration
- Bookbinding skill, or willingness to learn or work with a binder for the annual binding
- Familiarity with, or willingness to learn, the history of the Carta de Logu and Sardinian legal history generally
- An eye for which small details of a day will matter later — the discipline of a chronicler more than a journalist

**Explicitly not required**
- No performance or acting experience
- No involvement in, or opinion on, the barter mechanics of Act Two or Act Three beyond writing the board
- No technical equipment of any kind — pen, ink, and paper are the entire toolkit
- No obligation to finish the Carta de Logu copy within any fixed timeframe

**Boundaries of the role**
- Does not see or need to see any private project documentation beyond what's necessary to attend rehearsals
- Does not explain the record to the public; the pages, and later the volume, are left to speak for themselves
- Does not select, edit, or reorder the Carta de Logu text; copies it in sequence, as it stands
- Guardianship of the working pages ends at the binding; guardianship of the bound volume passes to the town

---

## VI. Why this role comes first

Every other role in Slow Theatre inherits the project's willingness to remain unexplained. The Remembrancer is what makes that willingness sustainable rather than simply invisible: without a record, unexplained becomes forgotten. With one, it becomes patient. That is why this role is named first among the satellite roles — not because it is more active than the others, but because it is what allows all of them, eventually, to be seen as having made something that lasted. The long task of the Carta de Logu extends that same patience outward, from La Prova's own history to Bosa's much older one.
