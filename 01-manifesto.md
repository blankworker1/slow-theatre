# Slow Theatre

### A philosophy of performance, in the tradition of Slow Food

---

## Where this comes from

In 1986, a protest against a fast food restaurant opening in Rome grew into something much larger: **Slow Food**, a movement built on the conviction that food should be *Good, Clean, and Fair* — and that the pace, scale, and industrial logic of "fast" was quietly destroying something worth protecting.

The same logic has since been applied to money (Slow Money), to cities (Cittaslow), to fish, to travel. Each time, the same three questions get asked of a different domain: *What has speed and scale cost us here? What would it mean to grow this slowly, locally, and on human terms? And what would "Good, Clean, and Fair" mean in this context?*

**Slow Theatre** asks these questions of performance.

---

## What "fast theatre" looks like

Theatre, like food, has its own industrial version: touring productions built for reproducibility rather than place; audiences positioned as consumers of a finished product; a hard line between performer and spectator; a season, a box office, a review, a close. Content that could play identically in any town, because it was never really *of* any town.

None of this is condemned outright — but it is a choice, and not the only one. Slow Theatre is the other choice.

---

## The three principles

### Good
A performance judged by how it feels to be *inside* it — the quality of attention, craft, and presence in the room — not by production values, audience size, or press coverage. Good means the work is worth doing well for its own sake, the way a good meal is worth cooking properly even if only a few people are at the table.

### Clean
No hidden commercial agenda, no extraction from participants, no gatekeepers deciding who is allowed to take part. Nothing here is branded, sold, or built to generate a return for someone standing outside the work. What happens belongs to the people in the room.

### Fair
Everyone present has equal standing. The line between performer and audience is deliberately blurred — not erased by force, but left open enough that anyone can step from watching into acting, and back again, without permission being asked or granted. The work is fair to *place*, too: it uses what is actually local — local stories, local people, local material — rather than something that could have happened anywhere.

---

## How it grows

Slow Food is about *what* is made. Slow Money is about *how it's paid for and how it grows* — patiently, locally, without chasing scale for its own sake. Slow Theatre borrows this too:

- **Patient, not urgent.** No opening night to build toward, no premiere to justify the effort. The work can be rehearsed, repeated, and left unfinished across many sittings, exactly like slow-grown produce or patient capital — value accrues over time, not in a single high-stakes moment.

- **Recurrence, not permanence.** Each performance is only ever *a* performance — one more rehearsal, not a final version. Nothing is trying to close, tour, or scale. Growth, if it happens, happens the way trust grows in a community: slowly, and only as fast as people are actually ready.

- **Local capital, not imported production.** The real "return" isn't ticket revenue or acclaim — it's the rebuilt capacity of a place to gather, speak, and make something together in public. That capacity is the soil being tended. Everything else is just evidence that the soil is healthy.

---

## What this looks like in practice

Slow Theatre isn't a format — it's a set of questions to ask before staging anything:

- Does this need an audience, or could everyone here be a participant?
- Does this need to be finished tonight, or can it be left open and picked up again?
- Is this material actually of this place, or could it happen anywhere?
- Who is this for, and who — quietly — is it *not* for? Has a gate been built where none needs to exist?
- What would it cost to do this well, slowly, without an outside sponsor or a schedule to justify?

Individual performances — each with their own name, structure, and rules — are simply answers to these questions, worked out in public. They are instances of this philosophy, not the philosophy itself.

---

## Why launch this at a Slow Food table

Slow Theatre doesn't ask to be taken on faith — it asks to be recognized. Anyone who has sat through a long, unhurried, locally-sourced meal already understands the logic being proposed here; they've simply never been invited to apply it to a performance. A Slow Food meal is the right room to make that invitation, because the argument has already been made once, in a language everyone at the table already trusts.
