# BosaTAZ — Fiscal

*Part of the TAZ folder. Bosa's own worked instance of the fiscal
boundary named in bosataz-boundaries.md — grounded specifically in
Italian civil law, not a claim about any other Node's legal
footing. This is a working reference, not legal advice: before
anything here is relied on for a formal ZAT application or a PPS
proposal, it needs review by someone actually qualified in Italian
law, not just a document that's been reasoned through carefully.*

## The legal basis

Three things move value or control at BosaTAZ, and Italian law already
has a name and a settled status for each, with no need for anyone's
permission:

- **Permuta** — barter, the direct exchange of one thing for
  another, with no money involved. This covers the Gallery: art for
  art, object for object.
- **Donazione** — gift, given freely, with nothing expected in
  return. This covers L'Usato, where nothing is asked for what's
  taken.
- **Detenzione** — custody without ownership. This covers the chain
  object: Il Custode holds it, uses it, protects it, without ever
  acquiring any claim to it. See its own section below — it works on
  a different legal mechanism entirely from the other two.

Neither permuta nor donazione requires registration, a licence, or a
gatekeeper's approval to be valid. Both are legally complete the
instant they happen — the same property that makes a promise in Act
Three valid the moment it's signed and posted, not when it's later
fulfilled. Nothing that happens at BosaTAZ needs anyone's permission
to already be real.

## The one rule that has to hold: no conguaglio

Permuta only keeps its status as barter — not a sale — if no cash
changes hands alongside the object. The moment a conguaglio (a cash
top-up) enters an exchange, Italian law starts treating it as a
partial vendita, a sale, with all the tax and reporting obligations
that follow.

This is why "no money crosses the boundary" is written into
BosaTAZ's design as a hard rule, not a preference: the Gallery's
zero-money principle isn't an aesthetic choice, it's the specific
legal condition permuta's status depends on. There's no soft
version of this rule — a small top-up isn't a small exception, it's
the whole legal footing changing underneath the exchange.

## The risk that scale creates: abitualità

Permuta and donazione are protected at the scale they're naturally
used at — occasional, non-recurring exchange between people who
aren't running it as a business. Regular, patterned exchange between
the same parties risks abitualità: reclassification as an undeclared
commercial activity, which loses the protection permuta and
donazione otherwise carry.

This is where BosaTAZ's temporary, non-persistent design is doing
real legal work, not just matching the project's aesthetic
commitments. A zone that appears once a rehearsal and dissolves
completely — no standing relay, no accumulating balance between the
same two people, nothing that persists between events — is close to
structurally immune to this risk, because "occasional, not repeated
between the same parties" is close to the actual legal test. The
truck packing down isn't just satisfying. It's the mechanism keeping
the whole arrangement legal.

## Custody without ownership: the chain object

The chain object doesn't move through permuta or donazione at all —
worth being precise about this, since neither article was written to
govern what actually happens to it. Both of those transfer
*ownership*. Custody of the chain object never does: it stays owned
by the collective the entire time, and Il Custode holding it changes
nothing about who it belongs to.

Italian law's own term for this is **detenzione** — physical holding,
recognised as legitimate, with no ownership claim attached. It
requires no contract and no paperwork of any kind: it arises purely
from *consegna*, a physical handover, backed by a *titolo* — a real,
recognisable reason the holding is happening — which the Remembrancer's
own chalk line already supplies in full: *"[Nome] ha in custodia
[oggetto], fino alla prossima Prova."* Not a legal document. A public,
witnessed statement doing exactly the job a titolo is required to do.

**Two things worth stating plainly, because they're the whole point of
this category rather than incidental features of it:**

- **No paper trail is ever required, at any stage.** Not at the first
  handover, not at the hundredth. The chalk line and the physical act
  of handing the object over are the entire mechanism, every time.
- **It can continue indefinitely, with no risk of quietly becoming
  ownership.** Only *possesso* can ever ripen into ownership over
  time, through years of genuinely acting as an owner would
  (*usucapione*). Detenzione never does, regardless of how long it
  continues, because the detentore explicitly recognises someone
  else's right the entire time — *animus detinendi*, not *animus
  possidendi*. Il Custode could hold the object for decades, across
  a hundred rotations, and be no closer to owning it on the last day
  than the first. This is what makes the mechanism sound
  indefinitely rather than only for a while: nothing about repetition
  or duration erodes it.

**The custody line is not a fourth promise format, and it's worth
being precise about why, since it sits on the board looking like one.**
The board's three promise formats — a good given (permuta-adjacent), a
service performed (prestazione-occasionale-adjacent), a thing lent and
owed back (comodato) — are all executory: commitments to a future act
that hasn't happened yet, written the moment the commitment is made.
The custody line describes something that has already happened — by
the time it's chalked, the handover already occurred, and Il Custode
is already holding the object. It's written in the present tense —
*"[Nome] **ha** in custodia [oggetto]"* — not as an offer, because it
isn't one.

This is the titolo referenced above, made explicit rather than
incidental: a promise creates a future obligation; a titolo evidences
a fact that's already true. The custody line does the second job. Even
its deadline phrasing shifts meaning accordingly — "fino alla prossima
Prova" doesn't mark a performance deadline the way "entro la prossima
prova" does for the other three; it marks how long an already-existing
state remains valid. Same shape of words, a genuinely different legal
function underneath.

## What the record is actually for

None of this needs a ledger to be legally valid — a permuta or
donazione is complete the instant it happens, witnessed or not. The
Remembrancer's chalk, BosaTAZ's seven layers of digital witnessing,
and the photograph taken before the board comes down aren't
compliance instruments. Nothing here is required by law.

What they're actually for is the promisee's side of Act Three,
which has no institutional backstop by design — no scribe, no
arbiter, no court standing behind a promise. The record exists so
someone a promise was made to has something to point to socially if
it goes unkept, not so an official has something to inspect. This
is worth being precise about, because it cuts the other way too: if
this record were ever treated as a formal ledger of taxable
transactions, that would undermine the very informality the fiscal
protection depends on. The record proves a promise was witnessed.
It was never meant to prove a transaction occurred in any sense the
tax code would recognize — there isn't one to prove.

**The record only ever obligates the Promittente — never the
Promissario.** Every example on the books states what's being given
and by when; none states what, if anything, comes back. This isn't
an omission — it's the design. A promise isn't a barter with a lag;
it's closer to a conditional, deferred gift, where "conditional"
means only "by a stated point," never "in exchange for something."
The record has no reason to track a return obligation, because the
Promissario never made one — recording an expectation nobody actually
committed to would mean witnessing something that hasn't happened,
which is exactly what the seven-layer structure exists never to do.
The freedom this creates is real, not incidental: whatever happens on
the Promissario's side of a promise — if anything ever does — stays
entirely outside the record, legally and completely, because there
was never anything there for the record to hold.

## The staged path, and what each stage actually needs

- **Ordinary Prova** — informal, run as often as needed, no
  application, no formal exposure beyond the underlying permuta and
  donazione protections already described above holding on their
  own merits.
- **ZAT** — a single, formal, publicized event, requiring an actual
  application to the Comune di Bosa. This is the first point where
  the reasoning above needs to hold up in front of someone who isn't
  inclined to take it on faith. The application's job is small and
  specific: permission for one temporary artistic-theatrical event,
  not a case for anything beyond it.
- **PPS** — only pursued after a ZAT has already run and succeeded.
  A different, later, larger proposal, made in collaboration with
  other local cultural groups, with its own legal grounding still to
  be worked out separately from what's written here.

## What this document doesn't cover

This file is scoped to the fiscal boundary as it governs exchange
inside BosaTAZ itself — permuta, donazione, detenzione, and the
conditions that keep each protected. It doesn't cover the ZAT
application's own administrative requirements, the eventual PPS
proposal's legal structure, or anything to do with Bosa™'s separate
cooperative corporate work, which runs on an entirely different legal
register and is deliberately kept unconnected to this one.
